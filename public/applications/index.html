01 / Gathering

Natural Gas Gathering

Gas gathering systems evolve with changes in well supply, production forecasts, delivery requirements and compression. Solvra is designed to help engineers study the connected network rather than treating each pipeline as an isolated calculation.

Natural gas network engineering →

Questions to investigate

  • How do changing supplies affect pressure and flow?
  • How might supported compressor or regulator settings change system behavior?
  • Which portions of the network merit further investigation under forecast cases?

02 / Planning

Midstream Pipeline Planning

Network planning involves new connections, changing throughput, operating constraints and potential infrastructure changes. Solvra is intended to help engineers compare modeled operating assumptions and understand their effects across the connected system.

Developing Engineering Studies workflows →

Questions to investigate

  • What happens when a new supply point is introduced?
  • How do deliveries respond to changed operating conditions?
  • What differences appear between proposed cases?

03 / Liquids

Crude Oil and Produced-Water Systems

For supported single-phase liquid networks, Solvra's steady-state modeling is intended to help engineers evaluate flow distribution, pressure and head behavior, pump operation and hydraulic losses across gathering and transfer systems.

Single-phase liquid pipeline modeling →

Questions to investigate

  • How do higher flow requirements change pressure and pump operation?
  • Where should a planner investigate hydraulic loss or pressure margins?
  • How do alternate operating cases compare?

A considered product direction

Other applications under consideration.

The initial market emphasis remains upstream and midstream. Additional applications depend on supported model assumptions and workflow validation.

04 / Potential application

Natural Gas Transmission

Transmission is another potential application for interconnected steady-state gas networks. Relevant workflows include evaluating supply and delivery changes, pressure profiles, supported compression and pressure-control conditions, and network operating alternatives.

05 / Emerging application

Natural Gas Distribution

Solvra's underlying gas network modeling approach may also be useful for distribution infrastructure. Utility-specific pressure-control and regulator workflows require further refinement and validation before distribution becomes an active commercial focus. The initial market emphasis remains upstream and midstream.

06 / Compare with context

Engineering Studies across applications.

Structured forecast, parametric and scenario comparisons are being developed to help engineers investigate how supported inputs change network results. The goal is repeatable analysis that connects assumptions and calculated outcomes.
