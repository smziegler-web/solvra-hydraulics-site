The engineering workbench

Model the entire network.

Solvra Hydraulics is a Windows desktop engineering application in development, combining geographic network construction, hydraulic simulation, engineering studies and result visualization in a modern workspace. Its initial commercial release will focus on steady-state analysis.

Actual pipeline systems bring together many supplies, deliveries, interconnected lines and operating components. Solvra is designed to evaluate these interactions as a connected system, so an engineer can investigate how changes in one area influence pressures and flows elsewhere.

01 / Build

Geographic Network Modeling

Build your network where your pipelines actually exist.

Solvra combines hydraulic network modeling with a georeferenced geographic workspace. Engineers can draw and edit networks against street maps or imagery basemaps and connect engineering data to real-world locations.

Mapped pipeline routes support automatic pipe-length calculation from drawn geometry. An elevation-lookup action can assist with populating model elevations where source data are available. These conveniences support model building; map-derived lengths and elevations still require engineering review where precision matters.

Geographic and GIS pipeline modeling

  • Georeferenced modeling with street and imagery basemaps.
  • Interactive network drawing and editing.
  • Automatic route-geometry-based pipe lengths.
  • Convenient elevation lookup, subject to data availability and accuracy.
  • GIS and supported shapefile import.
  • Pipeline, node and equipment properties in the same model.
  • Network visualization and engineering diagnostics.

02 / Gas

Natural Gas Network Engineering

Understand pressure, flow and operating conditions across a connected gas system.

Solvra is being developed for steady-state natural gas network analysis, with an initial emphasis on gathering and midstream planning. Engineers can model supplies and deliveries, pipe properties, compression and pressure regulation, and examine supported operating constraints throughout the network.

Core engineering areas include steady-state network pressure and flow, multiple supply/delivery locations, compressor and regulator controls, supported equipment limits, gas-property and composition calculations, and applicable thermal calculations with clearly identified modeling limitations.

03 / Liquids

Single-Phase Liquid Network Engineering

Bring oil and water networks into the same engineering workbench.

Solvra is also being developed for steady-state single-phase liquid systems, including crude oil gathering/transfer and produced-water gathering/transportation. Analyze pipeline pressure, flow and hydraulic head, supported pumps and their curves, valves, check valves and hydraulic loss elements throughout interconnected networks.

04 / Compare

Engineering Studies

Explore alternatives. Understand the consequences.

Operating conditions change, and an engineering decision rarely rests on a single calculation. Solvra's developing Studies workbench is intended to make comparisons of supported network conditions more organized and repeatable.

Forecast Studies

Investigate changing supplies and demands across a sequence of operating cases.

Parametric Studies

Compare results as supported engineering inputs change, using generated cases, results tables and charts.

Scenario evaluation

Examine alternate operating assumptions and their impact on network results.

These workflows are still undergoing development and integration ahead of the first commercial release. Supported inputs and equipment-variable coverage remain subject to integration and validation.

05 / Interpret

See the network behind the numbers.

Solvra is developing map-based presentation of pressure and flow, engineering tables, diagnostics and comparative study charts so engineers can interpret calculations in geographic and operating context. Clear identification of incomplete, infeasible or outdated results is an important design principle. Visualization workflows remain under active refinement.

06 / Connect

Work with existing engineering data.

GIS import and engineering data exchange help connect established network information to the modeling workbench.

Import Existing Network Models

Solvra Hydraulics includes import functionality for supported legacy Synergi Gas models, helping engineers bring existing network data into the modern Solvra Hydraulics modeling environment.

Import compatibility depends on the source model and supported features. Imported models should be reviewed and validated before engineering use.
